# West Cork (Skibbereen, Ballydehob, Schull, Bantry, Clonakilty)

- **Status:** ⚠️ likely fails our "6 dry months" climate rule (rain all year, guess — verify) · lead
- **Country / region:** Ireland, Co. Cork, south-west coast. Hub: **Skibbereen** (2,903 people, 2022). Villages: Ballydehob (~345), Schull, Bantry, Clonakilty, Glengarriff. [Wikipedia Skibbereen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skibbereen), [Wikipedia Ballydehob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballydehob)
- **Nearest town / airport:** Cork city + airport *~1 h 15 from Skibbereen (guess)*. Ballydehob is 13 km from Skibbereen and 13 km from Bantry.
- **Website / contacts:** [Skibbereen Farmers Market](https://skibbereenmarket.com/m-index/), [West Cork Sudbury School](https://www.westcorksudburyschool.ie/)
- **Added:** 2026-09-27 by Claude (web research)

## In short
Since the 1960s–70s, artists, craftspeople and "blow-ins" from England, Germany and Holland settled among small farms on the coast. Today: busy Saturday market, a democratic school, a traveller community on Coole Mountain, and wild coast with hills. **Ireland is EU → easy residency for both of us.** Downsides: rain, grey winters, and caravans on land need planning permission.

**Órgiva pattern:** 7/8 points — 1 market town ✅ (Skibbereen), 2 newcomers ✅, 3 spread out ✅, 4 projects ✅ (crafts, food, arts), 5 weekly market ✅, 6 alternative school ✅, 7 sea + hills ✅ (hills, not high mountains). Weak: 8 easy start ⚠️ (caravan rules, rental shortage *guess*).

## People
- 1960s–70s influx of artists, writers, craftspeople ("Ballydehob Bohemians"); cheap land and empty cottages drew them. [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/art-and-design/bohemian-blow-ins-crafting-an-arts-movement-in-west-cork-1.4205730), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballydehob)
- Coole Mountain "New Age Traveller" community ~200 people (2010). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coole_Mountain)
- *Spiritual side: yoga, crafts, food, some ceremony; more artsy than guru (guess).*

## Integration
- Irish farming and fishing towns. Blow-ins known locally for 50+ years. *Irish locals are usually friendly and chatty; pubs + GAA + music = social life (guess).*

## How we could join
- Volunteer on smallholdings / WWOOF *(common in West Cork, guess)*, rent, then buy.
- Caravan: ❌ living in a caravan/mobile home on land needs **planning permission**. Only storage (max 9 months/year, not lived in) is exempt. [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/life-and-style/homes-and-property/if-i-buy-a-plot-of-land-do-i-need-planning-permission-for-a-caravan-1.3507296), [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/property/homeandgardens/arid-40975916.html)

## Legal flags
- Home schooling: ✅ legal (Constitution Art. 42). Must register with **Tusla** (Section 14, Education (Welfare) Act 2000). [gov.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/policy-information/home-schooling-or-attendance-at-non-recognised-schools/), [HEN Ireland](https://henireland.org/what-is-home-education/legalities/)
- **West Cork Sudbury School** (Coomhola, between Bantry and Glengarriff), ages 5–18, since 2020; children registered via Tusla Section 14. [WCSS](https://www.westcorksudburyschool.ie/about-us)
- Van / yurt on land: ⚠️ planning permission.
- Wildfire risk: ✅ low *(spring gorse fires happen, guess)*.
- Expat bubble / mass tourism: ⚠️ summer tourism, holiday homes (Schull, Ballydehob).

## Water (honest view)
- ✅ Wet: west of Ireland ~1,000–1,400 mm/yr, rain on up to ~225 days a year. [Wikipedia Climate of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Ireland)

## Costs
- Farmland Cork (2025): good land <50 acres ~€17,250/acre; poor land ~€8,750/acre (= *~€21,600/ha poor land*). [Farmers Journal / SCSI-Teagasc 2026](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/news/news/average-price-for-good-agricultural-land-up-7-in-2025-913310), [Southern Star](https://www.southernstar.ie/subscriber-exclusives/cork-agri-land-second-dearest-in-munster-and-eighth-nationally-at-an-average-e17100-per-acre-4363850)
- *Houses with 1 ha: likely €250–400k (guess, not checked). Ireland has a housing shortage (guess).*
