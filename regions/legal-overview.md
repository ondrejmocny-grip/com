# Legal Overview (across countries)

> Checked: 2026-09-27 by Claude (web research). Some official pages were down, so some facts come from secondary sources. *Italic* = uncertain or a guess. Verify before acting.

## 1. Residency for the wife (Turkish citizen, spouse of an EU citizen)

**Main point:** She does **not** need a Czech residence permit first. When Ondrej moves to Spain or Portugal, she can get residency there **directly** as the spouse of an EU citizen (Directive 2004/38/EC).

- The EU Court (Metock, C-127/08, 2008) said a country **may not** require the spouse to have lived in another EU country first. [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A62008CJ0127)
- Condition: Ondrej must live there legally himself (work, self-employment, or enough money plus health insurance). [Directive 2004/38](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2004/38/oj/eng)
- A Czech national permit gives her only **visits** in other Schengen countries (90 days in any 180), not the right to live there. [IND](https://ind.nl/en/travelling-within-the-schengen-area-with-a-residence-permit-or-visa)

### Entry
- She needs a Schengen visa. As the spouse of an EU citizen she gets a **free, fast family visa** when she travels with Ondrej or joins him. [Spain MFA](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/Embajadas/dublin/en/ServiciosConsulares/Paginas/Consular/Visados-para-familiares-de-ciudadanos-de-la-Union.aspx), [SchengenVisaInfo](https://schengenvisainfo.com/eu-citizen-family-members/)
- Documents: her passport, a copy of Ondrej's passport, an **apostilled marriage certificate**, and proof that they travel together.

### Spain (incl. Canary Islands)
1. Enter together, or she joins Ondrej.
2. Ondrej registers as an EU resident (*certificado de registro*). *(usual order, guess)*
3. Within **3 months**, she applies **in person** with form **EX-19** at the Oficina de Extranjería or the police. [EX-19](https://www.inclusion.gob.es/documents/d/migraciones/ex19-formulario-tarjeta-de-residencia-de-familiar-de-ciudadano-de-la-ue-editable)
4. The receipt proves legal stay right away. The card is issued within 3 months. [administracion.gob.es](https://administracion.gob.es/en/tu-espacio-europeo/derechos-obligaciones/ciudadanos/residencia/obtencion-residencia/inscribir-familiares-no-ue)
- *5-year card, small fee (~€12), work allowed. (unverified)*

### Portugal
1. Ondrej registers at the Câmara Municipal (*certificado de registo*). *(usual order, guess)*
2. She applies **after 3 months, within the next 30 days**. She books through AIMA first.
3. Documents: Ondrej's EU ID, 2 photos, a certified passport copy, apostilled birth and marriage certificates, and a statement signed by Ondrej.
4. By law the card is issued within **6 months**. *AIMA has large backlogs.* [AIMA](https://aima.gov.pt/pt/nacionais-ue-e-familiares/familiares-de-nacionais-ue/cartao-de-residencia-para-familiares-de-nacionais-ue), [liveinpt](https://liveinpt.com/guides/article-15-aima-portugal/)

## 2. Home schooling

| Country | Status | Key conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Portugal | ✅ Legal (*ensino doméstico*) | Enroll at a school, which names a tutor. Parent needs the education level they teach. ⚠️ *News articles say since 2021 the teaching parent needs at least a **bachelor's degree** — conflicting sources, verify (see [portugal.md](portugal.md)).* Exams at the end of grades 4, 6 and 9. [DGE](https://www.dge.mec.pt/ensino-individual-e-ensino-domestico), [Portaria 69/2019](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/portaria/69-2019-120272926) |
| Italy | ✅ Legal (*istruzione parentale*) | Yearly notice to the school, plus a yearly exam. [D.Lgs 62/2017 art. 23](https://www.notiziedellascuola.it/legislazione-e-dottrina/indice-cronologico/2017/aprile/DLGS_20170413_62/cap4-art23) |
| Czechia | ✅ Legal (*individuální vzdělávání*, § 41) | Ask the school head. Teaching parent needs maturita (grades 1–5) or a degree (grades 6–9). Exams twice a year. [§ 41](https://www.pracepropravniky.cz/zakony/skolsky-zakon-uplne-zneni/paragraf-41/) |
| Spain (incl. Canaries) | ⚠️ Grey zone | No law allows it. School is compulsory from 6 to 16. Families risk cases with social services. [HSLDA](https://hslda.org/post/spain), [Waypoint](https://guides.waypointsur.com/homeschooling-spain/) |
| Greece | ❌ Illegal | Fines, and there were custody cases. [HSLDA](https://hslda.org/post/greece) |
| Turkey | ❌ Illegal for Turkish children | School enrollment is required. There is an "open school" option. [HSLDA](https://hslda.org/post/turkey) |

- *Idea to check:* a child with Czech citizenship living abroad may study in the Czech system, with exams at a Czech school.
- Alternative schools (Waldorf, free schools) may be a way around this in Spain. *To research per region.*

## 3. Van / yurt on private rural land

- **Spain:** By default you **may not live** on rustic land (*suelo rústico*). A van or yurt that stays on the plot is treated like a building. Rules depend on the region and the town. Legal routes: licensed rural tourism, or housing tied to a real farm. Fines are common. [yurteros](https://yurteros.com/legalidad-yurta-espana), [terrenos.es](https://terrenos.es/blog/vivir-en-una-caravana-en-terreno-rustico-es-posible)
- **Portugal:** Camping outside approved sites needs a license from the town council (DL 310/2002). Any fixed setup (foundations, water, power) needs a building license. The local land plan (PDM) decides what is allowed. A real case: a council stopped a family keeping caravans on their own farmland. *Some councils tolerate it in practice.* [postal.pt](https://postal.pt/economia/familia-de-nomadas-e-impedida-de-estacionar-as-caravanas-no-proprio-terreno-camara-municipal-alega-que-o-solo-e-agricola/)

**For us:** Joining an existing project that already has licenses (rural tourism or a farm) may be the easiest legal start.
